i(M)RL reads four ways:

Rules

  • Lowercase i, thin parentheses, bold M, R and L.
  • Always keep the parentheses.
  • Black on white, white on black, or black on yellow.
  • Never yellow on white (you can't read it).
  • Smallest size: 20px (the header).

Yellow always goes behind black text, never as text on white. Black on yellow passes contrast (14.7 : 1). Yellow on white fails (1.3 : 1).

DJ NO MO

Interactive Systems · Physical Computing · Arduino · Processing · 290 words

Add photo later!

Intro

DJ NO MO is an interactive music curator built to make music recommendation feel less like an algorithm and more like a person. As lead programmer and system assembler, I integrated all the hardware code, managed serial communication between Arduino and Processing, and made sure the whole thing actually worked.

Problem Statement

Spotify's DJ feature is clever, but falls flat: visually bland, cliché and predictable. We asked: what if music responded to your body and emotions instead of just your listening history?

User

We designed for three listeners: the Passive Listener, who wants effortless suggestions; the Expressive Mover, who craves a physical experience tied to mood; and the Control Freak, who wants to make the choice. Each shaped the input flow so every interaction felt purposeful.

Research / Design

Spotify's bold visual language showed us how colour, contrast and motion communicate mood instantly. That energy guided our vibrant genre discs and responsive Processing animations.

Solution

The flow was intentionally step-by-step: place a coloured disc to pick a genre, slide a control to set your mood, press buttons to answer short questions, then apply pressure to influence the result. Then the servomotor, LEDs and Processing visuals came alive. A ritual, not a form.

Results

At our class showcase, users completed the full loop without instruction. Feedback called it fun, surprising and more personal than Spotify.

Challenges

I built everything in Arduino first, then hit a wall connecting Processing and had to restart. Lesson: design the system before writing code. Running multiple sensors also lagged until we split the work: Arduino collects and transmits, Processing reads and renders.

Reflection

Inspired by my background in dance, I think about timing, flow and engagement when building systems. DJ NO MO is what I want to keep making: technology that feels human, responsive and alive.

Process analysis 02

ROLLO

2-week team jam · 4 designers · 2025 · Figma · UX/UI · 298 words

Add photo later!
(Fig. 01)ROLLO brand graphics

Intro

ROLLO is a playful mobile app concept that ends the “I don’t know, what do you wanna do?” loop. Four designers built it in a two-week design jam, taking it from concept to high-fidelity Figma prototype.

Problem Statement

Making plans with friends rarely goes smoothly. Decision fatigue kicks in fast: too many options, too much back-and-forth, and suddenly nobody goes anywhere. Existing planning apps were too complex, too serious or just not fun. Nobody was designing for spontaneity.

User

We focused on Gen Z social planners, people who want to make plans, not manage them. Competitive analysis and Gen Z design research uncovered three mindsets: the Overthinker, who needs something to decide for them; the Spontaneous One, who just needs a direction; and the People Pleaser, who wants it fun and fair for everyone.

Research / Design

Every visual choice had a reason: a bold, high-contrast palette like Spotify, Duolingo and BeReal, personality-forward sans-serif type, and a mascot inspired by Duolingo's. ROLLO should feel like a friend making a suggestion, not an algorithm.

Solution

One core interaction, done well: set your preferences, ROLLO randomizes a suggestion, you go.

Results

No hard metrics in a two-week jam, but the prototype communicates its value in under 10 seconds. Peer and mentor feedback highlighted how cohesive the visual system felt and how quickly the interaction made sense.

Challenges

Our first version tried to do everything at once: group syncing, filters, saved lists and randomizing. It did none of them well. The feedback was clear: every added feature made it feel more like the planning apps we were trying to replace. So we cut them.

Reflection

Scope is a design decision: committing to one focused interaction gave ROLLO its identity. And emotion is a feature. ROLLO could have been a list with a randomize button. The personality is what makes people connect.
